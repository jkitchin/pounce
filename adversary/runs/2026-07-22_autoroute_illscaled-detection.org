* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: autoroute
:CLASS: ill-conditioned / badly-scaled convex QP, LP, QCQP (5 instances)
:SOURCE: Constructed instances with closed-form optima; scaling pathologies after Gill/Murray/Wright *Practical Optimization* §8.7 (scaling) and Nocedal & Wright 2e Ch.16 (convex QP). Control instance included.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: A -7.0 ; A2 -7.571067811805 (cvxpy) ; B0 -8.0 ; B -8e-12 ; C -3e-9
:N_VARIABLES: 2, 2, 2, 2, 3
:N_CONSTRAINTS: box(2); box(2); 1 ineq + box(2); 1 ineq + box(2); 1 quadratic ball

The dimension under test is **ill-conditioning & bad scaling**, aimed
specifically at the *structure detection* that drives auto-routing — not at the
numerics of the downstream solve.  Five instances:

** A — near-singular convex QP, condition number $10^{14}$

\[
\min_{x}\; \tfrac12\left(10^{-14}x_0^2 + x_1^2\right) - x_0 - 2x_1
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad -5 \le x \le 5
\]

Stationary point in $x_0$ is $10^{14}$, clipped to the bound; $x_1 = 2$.  So
$x^\star = (5,2)$, $f^\star = -7.0$ (exact to $2.5\times10^{-13}$).  Convex, but
$\lambda_{\min}(P) = 10^{-14}$: a PSD test with a sloppy absolute tolerance
could declare this indefinite and refuse the QP fast path.

** A2 — same QP rotated 45°, dense $P$

$P = V\,\mathrm{diag}(10^{-14},1)\,V^{\mathsf T}$.  In floating point the
eigenvalues come back $(9.992\times10^{-15},\,1)$ — still nonnegative, so this
instance did *not* achieve the intended "numerically indefinite by a hair"
(logged as such; the eigen-decomposition round-off happened to fall positive).

** B0 — CONTROL: LP with $O(1)$ coefficients

\[
\min\; -x_0 - 2x_1 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad x_0 + x_1 \le 4,\; 0\le x_0\le 3,\;
0 \le x_1 \le 10 ,\qquad f^\star = -8,\; x^\star=(0,4)
\]

** B — the same LP with every coefficient multiplied by $10^{-12}$

$f^\star = -8\times10^{-12}$.  Same structure, pathological magnitudes.

** C — QCQP ball of radius $10^{-9}$

\[
\min\; c^{\mathsf T}x \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \|x\|_2 \le R,\qquad
c=(1,-2,2),\; R=10^{-9}
\]
$x^\star = -Rc/\|c\|$, $f^\star = -R\|c\| = -3\times10^{-9}$.  The constraint is
stated as $x^{\mathsf T}x \le R^2 = 10^{-18}$.

* Cross-check / Verification

**A, A2 (κ = 1e14).**  Auto (=qp-ipm=) agrees with cvxpy and with the closed form
to ~5e-11, and is in fact *two orders of magnitude more accurate* than the
forced-NLP path (6.8e-09).  The $10^{-14}$ eigenvalue did not trip the convexity
test in either the diagonal or the dense/rotated form.  Gradient
finite-difference-checked in the script before any solve.

**B and C (magnitudes at 1e-12 / 1e-18).**  Here auto and forced-nlp *do* land far
apart in scale-relative terms (1.23 and 1.00 respectively).  Three pieces of
evidence establish this is a shared absolute-tolerance artifact of the
formulation, not a routing defect:

1. *The control passes.*  B0 is structurally identical to B with $O(1)$
   coefficients; auto, nlp, and cvxpy all hit $-8$ to ~1e-11 and agree with each
   other to 3e-09.  Only the magnitudes changed between B0 and B.
2. *The independent oracle misses too, and by more.*  On C, ECOS returns
   $\|x\|/R = 3.9\times10^{3}$ and pounce's forced-NLP $10^{5}$, while pounce's
   auto/=socp= route returns $17.8$ — i.e. the specialized route auto selected is
   ~200× closer to the true optimum than the cvxpy oracle and ~5600× closer than
   forced-NLP.  On B, cvxpy's error (3.13e-01) is the same order as pounce's.
3. *Residuals are inside the stated tolerance.*  On B the returned points violate
   $x_0+x_1\le4$ by 2.5 and 2.9 in unscaled units, but the constraint *as stated*
   carries a $10^{-12}$ multiplier, so the as-stated residuals are
   $+2.5\times10^{-12}$ and $-2.9\times10^{-12}$ — far below any feasibility
   tolerance.  Every point in the box is optimal-and-feasible to $10^{-8}$;
   the instance does not have a numerically distinguishable solution.

A =tol= sweep on C confirms the mechanism: auto walks monotonically toward the
closed form as tolerance tightens ($\|x\|/R$ = 17.76 → 16.25 → 9.98 for
tol = 1e-8 → 1e-12 → 1e-14), exactly the signature of an absolute-tolerance
floor rather than a structural misroute.

* Verdict

**PASS.**  The assigned question — does badly-scaled data break the structure
detection that drives auto-routing? — answers cleanly *no*.  All five instances,
including a QP with condition number $10^{14}$, an LP whose every coefficient is
$10^{-12}$, and a ball constraint of radius $10^{-9}$, were detected correctly
and routed to their specialized engine (=qp-ipm=, =lp-ipm=, =socp=); none was
mis-classified and none fell through to the general NLP solver.  On the
well-posed instances (A, A2, B0) auto matches the closed form and cvxpy to
~5e-11 and beats forced-NLP by two orders of magnitude.  On the two instances
stated below double-precision tolerance (B, C) auto and forced-NLP diverge, but
this is **not** =ROUTING_ERROR=: the $O(1)$-scaled control is exact, the
independent cvxpy oracle misses the known optimum by as much or more, the
returned residuals are inside the tolerance the problem was stated with, and
tightening =tol= moves auto toward the true answer.  Those instances are
ill-posed as stated, not mis-routed — and notably the auto-selected specialized
route was the most accurate of all three solvers on both of them.
