#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Quadratic-over-linear (Cauchy-Schwarz) minimization
#+DATE: 2026-08-02

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: power
:CLASS: power-cone, quadratic-over-linear / hyperbolic constraint, fixed-constant coordinate
:SOURCE: classical weighted Cauchy-Schwarz minimization (quad-over-linear atom, cf. Boyd & Vandenberghe Sec 3.1.5); closed form via Lagrange multipliers, derived below
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 24.0
:N_VARIABLES: 6 (3 x_i, 3 epigraph t_i)
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1 equality (budget) + 3 power cones (alpha=0.5)
:SELECTED_BY: log-rotation
:COVERAGE_TARGET: -
:END:

\begin{align}
\min\quad & \sum_i x_i^2 / y_i \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_i x_i = C
\end{align}

with \(y=(1,2,3)\) FIXED constants, \(C=12\). By Lagrange multipliers,
\(x_i^* = C y_i / \sum_j y_j = (2,4,6)\), \(f^* = C^2/\sum y_j = 24\)
(equivalently, the equality case of weighted Cauchy-Schwarz).

Encoded via the power cone \(\{(u,v,w): |u|\le v^{0.5}w^{0.5}\}\), one triple
per \(i\): \((x_i, y_i, t_i)\), where \(y_i\) is a *constant* plugged
directly into \(h\) (its row of \(G\) is zero) rather than a free variable —
distinct from every previously-tested power/exp-cone problem in this log,
which always put a decision variable or the literal constant "1" in that
slot.

* Cross-check / Verification

A secondary observation, investigated but *not* filed: `x_inf_err` is
1.51e-4 versus the closed form, notably worse than the objective's 6.5e-8,
and CLARABEL's own `x` is exact to displayed precision on the identical
problem. Follow-up probes (in-session, not committed — reproducible from the
script above by varying `y`/`C`/`tol`):

1. Sweeping `tol` from 1e-8 down to 1e-11 changes *nothing* — identical
   `iters=8`, identical `x`, identical `kkt_error=1.803e-6`. The IPM
   genuinely stalls at that point; it isn't a case of "ask for more and get
   more," so a false-`optimal_inaccurate` explanation doesn't apply here
   (the status is `optimal`, not `_inaccurate`, at these tols).
2. Homogeneous `y=(1,1,1)` gets much better x-precision (2.18e-7) than
   `y=(1,2,3)` or `y=(2,2,2)` (both ~1.5-1.7e-4) at similar `kkt_error` —
   consistent with curvature: \(d^2/dx_i^2 [x_i^2/y_i] = 2/y_i\), so larger
   `y_i` flattens the objective near the optimum and the same
   ~1e-6 complementarity residual maps to a larger x-space error. This is a
   plausible, self-consistent *non-bug* explanation for most of the effect.
3. However, re-encoding the SAME problem with `y_i` as a free variable
   pinned via an extra equality constraint (`y_i = target`) instead of a
   constant baked into `h` recovers `x_inf_err = 6.1e-6` — roughly 25x
   better — at a comparable `kkt_error` (1.3e-6). Curvature alone (point 2)
   doesn't obviously explain a 25x gap between two encodings of an
   identical mathematical problem; this suggests the nonsymmetric nature
   or preconditioning path for a power-cone row whose coordinate is a pure
   constant (zero row of `G`) may be mildly under-conditioned relative to
   the same coordinate as a variable.

This is worth keeping an eye on (a coverage-style precision gap, not a
wrong-answer), but does not meet the SOLVER_BUG bar: status is honest, the
objective is correct to 1e-4 by a wide margin, and there is a plausible
partial explanation (curvature) even if it doesn't fully account for the
constant-vs-variable gap. Filing a full issue would need a sweep across
`y`-scales and cone dimensions to separate "expected curvature effect" from
"encoding-path precision gap" cleanly, which is out of scope for a single
adversary probe run.

* Verdict
PASS — objective matches the closed-form Cauchy-Schwarz optimum and cvxpy
(CLARABEL/SCS) to ~1e-7, well within the 1e-4 tolerance, with an honest
`optimal` status. Not filed: a secondary x-space precision observation
(constant-coordinate power-cone rows achieving less x-precision than the
same coordinate posed as a pinned variable, at matched kkt_error) is noted
above for a future, more targeted probe — it is a precision characteristic,
not a wrong answer, and current evidence doesn't yet isolate it from benign
curvature effects.
