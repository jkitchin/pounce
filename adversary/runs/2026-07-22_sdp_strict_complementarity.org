#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Degenerate SDPs -- strict-complementarity failure and a non-unique optimal face
#+DATE: <2026-07-22>

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: sdp
:CLASS: degeneracy / constraint-qualification / non-unique optima
:SOURCE: Hand-constructed instances in the standard degeneracy taxonomy of Alizadeh, Haeberly & Overton, "Complementarity and nondegeneracy in semidefinite programming", Math. Prog. 77 (1997) 111-128 (Defs. of primal/dual nondegeneracy and strict complementarity, Sec. 2-4); see also M. J. Todd, "Semidefinite optimization", Acta Numerica 10 (2001) 515-560, Sec. 4, on the loss of superlinear IPM convergence when strict complementarity fails. Optima derived analytically in closed form (below) -- no published numeric value is relied on.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: p* = 3 (variant A), p* = 1 (variant B); both exact
:N_VARIABLES: 3x3 symmetric X (4 free vars after elimination in A, 5 in B)
:N_CONSTRAINTS: one ("psd", 3) cone block (svec length 6); 2 linear equalities (A) / 1 (B) eliminated by parametrization
:END:

Both problems are in standard primal SDP form

\[
\min_X \; \langle C, X\rangle \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \langle A_i, X\rangle = b_i,\; X \succeq 0 ,
\]

and both are *strictly feasible in primal and dual* (Slater holds both sides,
so strong duality holds and there is no duality gap). The only pathology is
degeneracy -- which is precisely the regime where interior-point methods lose
superlinear convergence.

** Variant A -- strict complementarity FAILS

\[
C=\operatorname{diag}(3,0,1),\qquad
A_1=E_{11},\; b_1=1,\qquad
A_2=E_{22}+E_{13}+E_{31},\; b_2=0 .
\]

i.e. \(X_{11}=1\) and \(X_{22}+2X_{13}=0\).

*Primal.* \(X_{22}=-2X_{13}\ge 0 \Rightarrow X_{13}\le 0\); the \(\{1,3\}\)
minor gives \(X_{33}\ge X_{13}^2\). Hence
\(\langle C,X\rangle = 3+X_{33}\ge 3\), with equality iff \(X_{33}=0
\Rightarrow X_{13}=0 \Rightarrow X_{22}=0\), which (by PSD) zeroes row/column 2.

\[
p^\star = 3,\qquad X^\star=\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)\ \text{(unique)},\qquad \operatorname{rank}X^\star=1 .
\]

*Dual.* \(S=C-y_1A_1-y_2A_2=\begin{bmatrix}3-y_1&0&-y_2\\0&-y_2&0\\-y_2&0&1\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0\)
requires \(y_2\le 0\) and \(y_1\le 3-y_2^2\); maximizing \(b^\top y=y_1\) gives

\[
d^\star = 3,\qquad y^\star=(3,0)\ \text{(unique)},\qquad S^\star=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1),\qquad \operatorname{rank}S^\star=1 .
\]

\[
\operatorname{rank}X^\star+\operatorname{rank}S^\star = 1+1 = 2 \;<\; 3 = n
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \textbf{strict complementarity fails.}
\]

The direction \(e_2\) lies in the kernel of *both* \(X^\star\) and \(S^\star\).
Slater points exist on both sides (primal \(X=[[1,0,-0.1],[0,0.2,0],[-0.1,0,1]]\succ0\);
dual \(y=(-2,-1)\Rightarrow S\succ0\)), so this is a pure
strict-complementarity failure, not a Slater/gap pathology (that case was
covered separately on 2026-07-22).

** Variant B -- non-unique optimal set (a 2-dimensional optimal face)

\[
C=I+E_{33}=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,2),\qquad A_1=I,\; b_1=1 \;(\operatorname{tr}X=1).
\]

\(\langle C,X\rangle = \operatorname{tr}X + X_{33} = 1+X_{33}\ge 1\), so

\[
p^\star = 1, \qquad
\mathcal{F}^\star=\{X\succeq0:\operatorname{tr}X=1,\;X_{33}=0\},
\]

a whole 2-dimensional spectrahedral face (all trace-1 PSD matrices supported on
the leading 2x2 block) -- the optimizer is emphatically *not* a point. Dual:
\(S=C-yI\succeq0 \Rightarrow y\le1\), \(d^\star=1\), \(S^\star=\operatorname{diag}(0,0,1)\),
rank 1; max rank over the optimal face is 2, so \(2+1=3=n\) and strict
complementarity *does* hold here -- the pathology is pure non-uniqueness.
Consequently X is graded on OBJECTIVE and PSD FEASIBILITY only, never on
matrix equality.

** Encoding

=pounce.solve_socp= has no equality block, so in each variant X is parametrized
by free variables that satisfy the equalities identically and
\(\mathrm{svec}(X)=h-Gv\) forms the single =("psd",3)= block; the constant part
of the objective is added back by hand. The svec layout (lower triangle,
column-major, off-diagonals x sqrt(2)) is re-derived and asserted numerically in
the script via \(\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathrm{svec}(X)\cdot\mathrm{svec}(Y)\) and
\(\mathrm{smat}(\mathrm{svec}(M))=M\) on random symmetric matrices, and each
variant's \(G,h,c\) is asserted against the explicit matrix and objective at a
random test point.

** Narrative

pounce hits both analytic optima to ~3e-10 absolute and returns =optimal= in
both cases, in 2.3 ms and 1.1 ms -- faster than either oracle.

The interesting part is *how* variant A converges. pounce returns

: X = [[1, 0, -3.10e-5], [0, 6.21e-5, 0], [-3.10e-5, 0, 3.07e-10]]

The unique optimum is diag(1,0,0). The degenerate coordinates are off by
~3e-5 -- five orders of magnitude worse than the objective error -- and they
sit in exactly the expected relationship: \(X_{22}=-2X_{13}\) (a feasibility
identity), and \(X_{33}\approx X_{13}^2\) (3.07e-10 vs 9.6e-10). That is the
textbook \(O(\sqrt{\mu})\) signature of strict-complementarity failure: the
coordinate in \(\ker X^\star\cap\ker S^\star\) converges only at the square
root of the barrier parameter, so the objective (which sees it quadratically)
converges quadratically faster than the iterate. Both cvxpy oracles show the
same phenomenon. This is a property of the problem, not a defect in pounce.

The iteration counts corroborate it: 14 iterations for the degenerate variant A
versus 7 for variant B, consistent with the loss of superlinear local
convergence predicted by Todd (2001) Sec. 4 when strict complementarity fails.

Variant B's non-unique face causes pounce no trouble at all. It returns the
"analytic-center"-like point diag(0.5, 0.5, 0) -- the maximum-rank (rank 2)
element of the optimal face, which is exactly what a primal-dual central-path
method should converge to. Any point on that face is equally optimal, hence
grading on objective + PSD feasibility rather than on X.

* Cross-check / Verification
- Two independent oracles (cvxpy 1.x with SCS and CLARABEL) on the *natural*
  matrix formulation (=cp.Variable((3,3), symmetric=True)= with =X >> 0= and the
  equalities written directly), not on the eliminated parametrization pounce
  sees. They agree with each other to < 1.5e-8 in both variants, and with the
  closed-form optima.
- Both closed-form optima are re-derived above from PSD minors alone and
  confirmed by explicit dual certificates \((y^\star, S^\star)\) with
  \(\langle C,X^\star\rangle = b^\top y^\star\) and \(X^\star S^\star = 0\).
- svec layout re-derived from scratch and asserted numerically before any
  comparison was made (5 random symmetric pairs, both
  \(\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathrm{svec}\cdot\mathrm{svec}\) and the smat round
  trip), plus per-variant assertions that \(h-Gv=\mathrm{svec}(X(v))\) and
  \(c^\top v+\text{const}=\langle C,X(v)\rangle\) at a random \(v\).
- PSD feasibility of pounce's returned X checked by =eigvalsh=: -6.6e-10
  (variant A) and +3.1e-10 (variant B).

* Verdict
=PASS= -- pounce solves both degenerate SDPs to ~3e-10 objective accuracy, in
agreement with SCS and CLARABEL and with the analytic optima, while returning
numerically PSD iterates and =optimal= status, in ~1-2 ms (faster than both
oracles). Strict-complementarity failure (variant A) shows up only as the
expected \(O(\sqrt{\mu})\) degradation of the *iterate* in the doubly-degenerate
coordinate (~3e-5) and a doubling of the iteration count -- both are intrinsic
to the problem class and reproduced by both oracles, not solver defects. A
non-unique 2-dimensional optimal face (variant B) is handled cleanly, with
pounce returning the maximum-rank element of the face as a central-path method
should. No issue filed.
