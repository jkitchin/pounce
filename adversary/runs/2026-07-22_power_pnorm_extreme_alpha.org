#+TITLE: Adversary Run: p-norm minimization at EXTREME power-cone alpha (p=100 and p=1.01)
#+DATE: 2026-07-22

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: power
:CLASS: ill-conditioning / bad scaling -- power-cone barrier at alpha -> 0 and alpha -> 1
:SOURCE: Analytic Lagrange/Hoelder solution -- Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization (2004) Ex. 4.7 / App. A.1.6 (dual-norm characterization); MOSEK Modeling Cookbook v3.3 sec. 4.2 (power-cone epigraph |x|^p <= s).
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 1.112947785269e+01 (p=100) ; 9.987788604526e-01 (p=1.01)
:N_VARIABLES: 8 (p=100: 4 x + 4 s) ; 6 (p=1.01: 3 x + 3 s)
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1 equality + 4 (resp. 3) 3-D power cones
:END:

Minimize the p-th power sum over a hyperplane:

\[
\min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^n} \; \sum_{i=1}^n |x_i|^p \quad \text{s.t.} \quad a^\top x = b .
\]

With Hoelder conjugate \(q = p/(p-1)\) and \(S = \sum_i |a_i|^q\), stationarity
\(p|x_i|^{p-1}\mathrm{sgn}(x_i) = \lambda a_i\) gives the closed form

\[
x_i^* = \mathrm{sgn}(a_i)\,|a_i|^{q-1}\,(b/S), \qquad f^* = (b/S)^p\,S .
\]

** Why this is the ill-conditioning probe

The epigraph \(|x_i|^p \le s_i\) encodes as \((x_i, s_i, 1) \in K_{1/p}\), so the
cone exponent is \(\alpha = 1/p\). The two instances sit at the two ends of the
legal range \(\alpha \in (0,1)\):

- \(p = 100 \Rightarrow \alpha = 0.01\): \(K_\alpha\) degenerates toward the
  \(z\)-axis half-line.
- \(p = 1.01 \Rightarrow \alpha = 0.990099\): \(K_\alpha\) degenerates toward the
  \(y\)-axis; the objective is nearly \(\ell_1\) and the optimum concentrates on
  the largest \(|a_i|\), an L1-like near-vertex.

In both regimes the barrier
\(F = -\log(y^{2\alpha}z^{2-2\alpha} - x^2) - (1-\alpha)\log y - \alpha\log z\)
has wildly asymmetric curvature in \(y\) versus \(z\).

** Data scaling (deliberate)

Both instances are scaled so the *analytic optimum stays O(1)*, so any observed
error is attributable to the barrier/IPM rather than to float overflow in the
reference:

- \(p=100\): \(a = (2,-3,1,5)\), \(b = S\) so that \(b/S = 1\), \(f^* = S\),
  \(x_i^* = \mathrm{sgn}(a_i)|a_i|^{1/99} \approx 1\).
- \(p=1.01\): \(a = (1,-0.98,0.5)\), \(b = 1\). Here \(q = 101\), so \(|a_i| \le 1\)
  is *required* to keep \(S\) finite. Reusing \(a=(2,-3,1,5)\) at \(p=1.01\)
  gives \(S \approx 4\times10^{70}\); pounce then returns =numerical_failure=,
  but that is a property of 1e70-scaled data, not of the cone. This script
  avoids that trap on purpose and the sweep below keeps the two arms separate.

The x inf-error is 6.87e-04, three orders larger than the objective error. This
is *intrinsic problem flatness, not solver error*: at p -> 1 the objective is
nearly \(\ell_1\), so the optimum is nearly degenerate along the constraint and a
7e-4 displacement in x costs only 4e-7 in objective. Direct evidence: the two
CLARABEL runs of the *same* problem (via =cp.power= and via explicit
=PowCone3D=) disagree with each other by 1.10e-04 in x while agreeing to 4e-09
in objective. The oracle cannot pin x any better than pounce can.

* Cross-check / Verification

** Cone convention (verified read-only, not assumed)

Confirmed against =crates/pounce-convex/src/cones/power.rs=:
=K_alpha = {(x,y,z) : |x| <= y^alpha z^(1-alpha), y,z >= 0}=, and
=PowerCone::new= asserts =0 < alpha < 1= — so alpha = 0.01 and 0.990099 are
both legal, and neither extreme is clamped or rejected. In
=python/pounce/qp.py::solve_socp=, =("pow", alpha)= carries the EXPONENT, not a
dimension, and the slack =s = h - Gx= must lie in the cone. Hence
\(|x_i|^p \le s_i \iff (x_i, s_i, 1) \in K_{1/p}\), i.e. alpha = 1/p.

The permutation trap was checked explicitly: cvxpy's
=PowCone3D(u,v,w,alpha)= means \(u^\alpha v^{1-\alpha} \ge |w|\), i.e.
pounce \((x,y,z)\) = cvxpy \((w,u,v)\). Each case was built BOTH via the DCP
=cp.power= atom and via the permuted =PowCone3D=, and both reproduce the closed
form — so the pounce encoding is confirmed rather than coincidentally matching a
wrong convention.

** Three independent oracles

Closed-form Hoelder solution + CLARABEL (DCP) + SCS (DCP) + CLARABEL (explicit
power cone) all agree with pounce to <= 4.1e-06 relative in both cases. The
analytic x* is asserted feasible to 1e-9 before each solve.

* Verdict

*PASS.* pounce solves both extreme-alpha instances correctly: 4.12e-06 relative
objective error at alpha = 0.01 and 3.92e-07 at alpha = 0.990099, agreeing with
the closed-form Hoelder solution and with three independent cvxpy oracles, in
under 7 ms each. At alpha = 0.01 pounce is actually more accurate than the SCS
oracle. The cone encoding was verified read-only against =power.rs= and
cross-checked against both the DCP and the explicitly-permuted =PowCone3D=
oracle forms, so this is a confirmed match and not a coincidence of conventions.

Two behaviors are documented but are *not* findings. First, the status is
=optimal_inaccurate= rather than =optimal= in both cases and does not respond to
tighter =tol= or larger =max_iter=; this is the previously-logged, accepted
reduced-accuracy acceptance behavior on power-cone problems (see the p=1.5
2026-06-15 and p=2.5 2026-07-19 entries, both logged PASS with the same status),
and it is not specific to extreme alpha — the sweep shows it at p=2 and p=3 as
well. Second, the alpha -> 0 arm loses roughly two orders of objective accuracy
relative to moderate p (5e-08 -> 4e-06) and needs about twice the iterations;
this is a real and cleanly monotone barrier-conditioning effect worth knowing
about, but it stays well inside the 1e-4 bar and pounce reports the reduced
accuracy honestly rather than falsely certifying optimality. The one genuine
=numerical_failure= observed came from a badly scaled *reference* (a=(2,-3,1,5)
at q=101 puts the data at 1e70), which is a formulation hazard of the test, not
a solver defect.
