#+TITLE: Adversary Run: MPCC Complementarity NLP — MFCQ/LICQ Failure, Unbounded Multiplier Ray
#+DATE: 2026-07-22

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: nlp
:CLASS: degenerate / constraint-qualification failure / non-unique optima / unbounded multiplier set
:SOURCE: Scheel & Scholtes, "Mathematical Programs with Complementarity Constraints: Stationarity, Optimality, and Sensitivity", Math. of OR 25(1):1-22, 2000, Sec. 2; Fletcher & Leyffer, "Solving MPECs with NLP solvers", Optim. Methods Softw. 19(1):15-40, 2004, eqs. (2)-(3).
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 1.0
:N_VARIABLES: 2
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1 nonlinear inequality + 2 lower bounds
:END:

\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^2}\ (x_1-1)^2+(x_2-1)^2
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x_1\ge 0,\ \ x_2\ge 0,\ \ x_1 x_2 \le 0 .
\]

The feasible set is the union of the two non-negative coordinate half-axes.

** Analytic facts (why this is the right degeneracy probe)
- *Two* global minimizers, \(f^*=1\) exactly: \(x^*=(1,0)\) and \(x^*=(0,1)\).
  Non-unique primal optimum.
- At \(x^*=(1,0)\) the active constraints are \(-x_2\le 0\) and \(x_1x_2\le 0\),
  with gradients \((0,-1)\) and \((x_2,x_1)=(0,1)\) — *parallel and opposite*.
  LICQ fails (rank 1 < 2 actives). MFCQ fails: no \(d\) satisfies both
  \(-d_2>0\) and \(-d_2<0\). This is the classic MPCC degeneracy — *no* CQ of
  MFCQ strength holds at either solution.
- KKT stationarity at \((1,0)\):
  \((0,-2)+\lambda_1(0,-1)+\lambda_2(0,1)=0 \Rightarrow \lambda_2-\lambda_1=2\).
  The multiplier set is the *unbounded ray* \(\{(t,\,t+2):t\ge 0\}\). KKT points
  exist, but the multipliers are non-unique and the set is unbounded — exactly
  the pathology under test.
- Relaxed family \(x_1x_2\le\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon>0\), restores MFCQ
  (Slater holds) and was swept to see whether the dual blows up as
  \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\).

** Multipliers — the point of the test
pounce does *not* claim `optimal` at a point where multipliers fail to exist.
Multipliers *do* exist here (the KKT set is nonempty), they are merely
non-unique and unbounded. pounce returns \(\lambda_{\rm comp}\approx 2.2479\),
which sits squarely on the theoretical ray \(\lambda_2=\lambda_1+2\) with
\(\lambda_1=t\approx 0.248\) picked up on the \(x_2\ge0\) bound — i.e. the
interior-point method selects a *finite, well-centered* element of the
unbounded ray rather than diverging. Ipopt selects essentially the same element
(magnitude 2.2479, matching to 5+ digits). No dual divergence, no
`dual_infeasible`, no restoration failure, no iteration-limit exit.

The relaxed sweep confirms the dual does *not* blow up as \(\varepsilon\to0\):
it settles near 2.02–2.25 rather than diverging, because the barrier picks the
near-minimum-norm ray element. (The intermediate \(\varepsilon=10^{-3}\) dual of
61 belongs to a *different*, symmetric stationary point — see below — not to a
blow-up.)

* Cross-check / Verification
- *Ipopt oracle (Pyomo, identical model):* agrees on objective to ~1e-9 at all
  8 cases and lands on one of the two known global minimizers each time. On two
  of the four degenerate starts pounce and Ipopt pick *different* (but equally
  valid) global minimizers — expected for a problem with non-unique optima, not
  a disagreement.
- *=pounce verify= on the degenerate .nl (solver-independent KKT/feasibility
  oracle):* `rc=0`, `VERDICT: VERIFIED`. Reported max constraint violation
  9.97e-9, KKT stationarity residual 1.22e-8, complementarity residual 2.24e-8,
  objective 0.99999998006. The claimed point is genuinely feasible and
  genuinely stationary — pounce's `optimal` claim is backed by an independent
  check.
- *Bare-CLI timing on the same .nl:* pounce 0.010s vs Ipopt 0.020s wall
  (pounce's own reported solve time 0.004s, 25 iterations). The 20-30x gap
  visible in the Pyomo-path table is *pyomo-pounce plugin/startup overhead*,
  not solver time. No PERFORMANCE_REGRESSION.

** Secondary observation (out of scope for this dimension, not filed)
The `pyomo-pounce` plugin reports `<=`-constraint duals with the *opposite sign*
to Ipopt under Pyomo's `Suffix.IMPORT`. Reproduced on a trivial non-degenerate
QP (min \((x-3)^2+(y-3)^2\) s.t. \(x+y\le2\), \(x-y=0\)): both solvers return
\(x=y=1\), pounce `dual_c = +4.0`, Ipopt `dual_c = -4.0`. This is a general
convention difference, not a degeneracy artifact. Magnitudes always match.
`pounce verify` is sign-agnostic (it prints `dual sign +1` after auto-detecting)
so it does not catch this. Worth a separate look; it is not a correctness
failure of the solver core.

** Secondary observation: local minima in the relaxed family
For \(\varepsilon=10^{-1}\) and \(10^{-3}\) *both* pounce and Ipopt converge from
the symmetric start (0.5,0.5) to the symmetric stationary point
\(x_1=x_2=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\), which is *not* globally optimal for the relaxed
problem (e.g. at \(\varepsilon=10^{-1}\), \((1,0.1)\) gives \(f=0.81<0.935\)).
Both local solvers agree exactly, so this is expected local-solver behavior on a
non-convex feasible set, not a pounce defect.

* Verdict
*PASS* — On the canonical MPCC degeneracy, where LICQ and MFCQ both fail at both
global minimizers and the multiplier set is a genuinely unbounded ray, pounce
converges in 25 iterations to a global minimizer from all four starts, matches
the published \(f^*=1\) to 2e-8 and the nearest \(x^*\) to 1e-8, matches Ipopt to
~1e-9, and is independently confirmed by `pounce verify` (rc=0, VERIFIED,
stationarity residual 1.2e-8). It reports a finite, well-centered element of the
unbounded multiplier ray (\(\lambda\approx2.25\), consistent with the analytic
relation \(\lambda_2-\lambda_1=2\)) rather than diverging, and does not raise a
spurious dual-divergence / infeasibility / restoration flag. The `optimal` claim
is *not* made at a point where multipliers fail to exist — multipliers exist
here, they are just non-unique — so there is no false optimality claim. pounce
is also faster than Ipopt at the bare-CLI level (0.010s vs 0.020s). The only
anomaly found is a dual *sign-convention* mismatch in the Pyomo plugin, which is
orthogonal to this dimension and not a solver-core defect.
