:CLASS: strictly-complementary convex QP, equality + inequality constrained
:SOURCE: Analytic. Envelope theorem: Fiacco, /Introduction to Sensitivity and Stability Analysis in NLP/ (1983), Thm 3.2.2; Boyd & Vandenberghe, /Convex Optimization/, §5.6.3 (local sensitivity: dp*/du_i = -lambda_i*, dp*/dv_i = -nu_i*). Implicit differentiation of the KKT system: Amos & Kolter, /OptNet/, ICML 2017, eqs. 6-8.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: obj* = -3.124705568267e-01 (self-consistent; the oracle here is the derivative identities, not the objective value)
:N_VARIABLES: 4
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1 equality, 4 inequalities (2 strictly active, 2 strictly inactive)
:END:

\begin{align*}
\min_{x \in \mathbb{R}^4} \quad & \tfrac{1}{2} x^\top P x + c^\top x \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & A x = b \quad (A = [1\,1\,1\,1],\ b = 1) \\
                  & G x \le h
\end{align*}

with \(P = LL^\top + 1.5 I\) (SPD, seeded RNG, \(\kappa(K_{\text{KKT}}) = 1.17\times10^{2}\)),
\(c = (-1,\,0.7,\,-2,\,0.3)\), and \(G,h\) chosen so that rows 0 and 1 are
strictly active and rows 2 and 3 strictly inactive.

The design goal was *strict complementarity*, which is what makes
\(x^*(\theta)\) differentiable at all. Measured at the solution:

No weakly active row, margins of order 1 on both sides — so any derivative

* Cross-check / Verification

Three mutually independent oracles were used; none of them re-runs pounce's
own differentiation code path.

1. *Envelope theorem.* \(\partial \mathrm{obj}^*/\partial h = -z\) and
   \(\partial \mathrm{obj}^*/\partial b = -y\) were compared directly against
   the multipliers =res.z= / =res.y= returned by the *host* =pounce.qp.solve_qp=
   — a different entry point from the layer. Agreement to ~1e-12 means the
   layer's gradient and the solver's reported duals are the *same* quantity;
   a sign flip or a scale error in either would have shown up immediately.
   Note the layer correctly returns exactly zero gradient on the two inactive
   rows, matching \(z_i = 0\).

2. *Hand-built implicit differentiation.* The reduced KKT matrix
   \(K = \begin{bmatrix} P & A^\top & G_\mathcal{A}^\top \\ A & 0 & 0 \\ G_\mathcal{A} & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\)
   was assembled in the script from the reported active set and multipliers,
   and \(dx\) obtained by =numpy.linalg.solve= for each perturbation direction
   (dP, dc, dA, db, dG, dh), with the correct multiplier-dependent right-hand
   sides \(-(dP\,x + dc + dA^\top y + dG^\top z)\), \(db - dA\,x\),
   \(dh_\mathcal{A} - dG_\mathcal{A}x\). This reconstruction reproduces every
   JAX and Torch gradient (including the full matrix gradients w.r.t. P, G, A)
   to ~1e-12. It is the sharpest of the three checks because it uses the
   *reported duals* as inputs — it would fail if the duals were wrong even if
   the layer were self-consistent.

3. *Central finite differences, float64, step sweep* over {1e-4, 1e-5, 1e-6}.
   Sweep spread was 3.6e-10 or better on every parameter, confirming the FD
   estimates sit on a clean plateau (no truncation/roundoff contamination);
   the FD values agree with both the multipliers and the IFT reconstruction.
   The P finite difference used a *symmetric* perturbation \(E_{ij}+E_{ji}\)
   (halved on the diagonal) to match the documented symmetric-gradient
   convention.

4. *JAX vs Torch parity.* All twelve gradient tensors (6 parameters x 2 losses)
   agree *bitwise* (max abs difference exactly 0.0), which is expected given
   both front ends dispatch to the same Rust backward.

5. *torch.autograd.gradcheck / gradgradcheck.*
   - =gradcheck(c, h, b, G, A)= -> True
   - =gradcheck(P = 0.5*(M + Mᵀ), c, h, b)= -> True
   - =gradcheck(raw P)= -> GradcheckError, *expected and not a defect*:
     gradcheck perturbs each \(P_{ij}\) independently (asymmetrically), whereas
     the layer documents \(\nabla P\) as the symmetrized gradient
     \(\tfrac12(d_x x^\top + x d_x^\top)\) because the solver reads only P's
     lower triangle. Routing P through an explicit symmetrizer makes the chain
     rule reconcile the two conventions and gradcheck passes. This is exactly
     what =python/pounce/jax/_qp.py='s module docstring warns about ("perturb
     ``P`` symmetrically when checking it against finite differences"), and the
     hand-built symmetric-perturbation FD in check (d) confirms the symmetric
     gradient is the numerically correct one. Documented, tested, not a bug —
     though it is the single most likely source of a *false* GRADIENT_ERROR
     report by anyone running gradcheck naively on this layer.
   - =gradgradcheck(c, h, b)= -> True (second-order through the implicit VJP
     also holds).

* Verdict

*PASS*. On a QP constructed to satisfy strict complementarity with order-1
margins, the differentiable layer's gradients, the solver's reported KKT
multipliers, an independently hand-assembled implicit-differentiation
reconstruction, and central finite differences all agree to 1e-11 or better,
and JAX and Torch agree bitwise. The envelope identities
\(\partial \mathrm{obj}^*/\partial h = -z\) and
\(\partial \mathrm{obj}^*/\partial b = -y\) hold exactly with pounce's reported
sign convention (\(L = f + y^\top(Ax-b) + z^\top(Gx-h)\), \(z \ge 0\)), which
means the duals and the sensitivity machinery are the same object viewed two
ways — no localization of a defect was possible because there was no
discrepancy to localize. The only "failure" observed, raw-P
=torch.autograd.gradcheck=, is a mismatch between gradcheck's asymmetric
perturbation and the layer's documented symmetric-gradient convention, and it
disappears when P is routed through an explicit symmetrizer.
