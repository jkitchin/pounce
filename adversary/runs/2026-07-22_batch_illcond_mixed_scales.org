#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Batch of QPs with Mixed Conditioning and Objective Scales
#+DATE: 2026-07-22

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: batch
:CLASS: ill-conditioning & bad scaling (per-item heterogeneous)
:SOURCE: internal-consistency contract (batch == per-item single solve_qp) + cvxpy/CLARABEL,SCS,OSQP,ECOS per item + EXACT exhaustive active-set enumeration over the box (3^6 = 729 patterns)
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: per item, from exact enumeration; hard item k=5: -6.8510532741e+19
:N_VARIABLES: 6 per item x 6 items = 36
:N_CONSTRAINTS: box lb=-1, ub=+1 on every variable (12 bounds per item)
:END:

Six box-constrained convex QPs solved as one batch:

\[
\min_x\ \tfrac12 x^\top P_k x + c_k^\top x \quad \text{s.t.}\quad -1 \le x \le 1,
\qquad k = 0,\dots,5
\]

with \(P_k = s_k\, Q_k \operatorname{diag}(\lambda)\, Q_k^\top\), \(Q_k\) random
orthogonal, \(\lambda\) log-spaced over \([1, 10^{2k}]\), and objective scale
\(s_k = 10^{2.4k}\). So item 0 has \(\kappa(P)=10^0\) and objective \(O(1)\),
while item 5 has \(\kappa(P)=10^{10}\) and objective \(O(10^{19})\) --- a
\(10^{10}\) conditioning spread and a \(\sim 10^{19}\) objective-magnitude
spread inside a single =solve_qp_batch= call sharing one =tol=.

Each \(c_k = -P_k x^{\text{target}}_k\) with \(x^{\text{target}}\) outside the
box, so the active set is nontrivial (several bounds active at the optimum).

* Cross-check / Verification

** The assigned hypothesis is REFUTED
The specific risk under test --- a batch-level *shared* tolerance or shared
scaling that is right for one item and silently wrong for another --- does not
occur:

- =batch vs per-item single solve_qp=: per-item max rel-x = *0.00e+00*, bit-for-bit
  identical on all six items, including the ill-conditioned one.
- =mixed batch vs batch-of-1 (solo)=: per-item drift = *0.00e+00* on all six.
  Putting a \(10^{12}\)-scaled item next to a \(10^0\)-scaled item does not
  perturb either. There is no cross-item contamination and no batch-global
  scaling.
- The shared =tol= propagates per item correctly: =tol=1e-12, max_iter=500=
  moves items 0-4 by <= 3e-8 (already converged) and moves item 5 by 5.85e-01
  (still converging), which is the expected per-item behavior.
- Statuses are attributed to the right index: =['optimal' x5, 'iteration_limit']=
  in both the batch and the per-item single path.

** Independent oracles agree with each other, not with pounce, on item 5

pounce's objective is *lower* than the true optimum only because its returned
iterate is grossly *infeasible*: it violates the box by 0.884 on at least one
coordinate. It is not a better solution, it is an unconverged interior-point
iterate.

** pounce does reach the right answer with a larger budget
| max_iter (tol=1e-10) | status          | rel-x vs exact | box viol |
|----------------------+-----------------+----------------+----------|
|                  200 | iteration_limit |       5.72e-01 | 5.72e-01 |
|                 1000 | iteration_limit |       5.75e-01 | 5.75e-01 |
|                 5000 | iteration_limit |     *5.04e-09* | 5.04e-09 |

At =max_iter=5000= pounce lands on the exact enumeration optimum to 5e-9 ---
so the algorithm is correct, it just needs ~1000x the iterations CLARABEL/OSQP
need at \(\kappa = 10^{10}\). Note that even at 5000 iterations the status is
still reported as =iteration_limit= despite the point being converged to 5e-9,
i.e. the termination test never fires at this conditioning.

* Verdict

*SOLVER_LIMITATION.* The batch machinery itself is clean --- this run found no
shared-tolerance or shared-scaling defect, and that was the hypothesis being
tested. Batch results are bit-for-bit identical to per-item single solves and
to batch-of-1 solves, so batching neither degrades nor contaminates a
badly-scaled item.

The finding is in the underlying convex QP IPM, and it surfaces at
\(\kappa(P) \approx 10^{10}\) with \(O(10^{19})\) objective magnitude: pounce
exhausts its default iteration budget and returns a point that violates the box
constraints by 0.884 (rel-x error 8.8e-1 vs the exact optimum), on a problem
that CLARABEL, OSQP, ECOS and SCS all solve at default settings in milliseconds.
Two mitigating facts keep this a limitation rather than a bug: (1) the status is
honestly reported as =iteration_limit=, never =optimal=, so a caller checking
status is not misled by a silently wrong answer --- which is exactly the failure
mode this iteration was hunting; and (2) =max_iter=5000= recovers the exact
optimum to 5e-9, so the iteration is convergent, merely slow. The secondary
observation --- that the =optimal= termination test still does not fire even
once the iterate is accurate to 5e-9 --- suggests the convergence test is
measured in unscaled units and becomes unsatisfiable when the objective is
\(O(10^{19})\); that is the more likely root cause and the more actionable one.
