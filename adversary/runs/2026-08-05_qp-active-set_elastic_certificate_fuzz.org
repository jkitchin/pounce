#+TITLE: Adversary Run: l1-elastic infeasibility certificate under nonconvex step QPs
#+DATE: <2026-08-05>

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: qp-active-set
:CLASS: randomized nonconvex QP, feasibility-certificate soundness
:SOURCE: property-based; instances generated with a constructive verdict, adjudicated by scipy.optimize.linprog (HiGHS)
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: n/a — the property under test is the *status*, not the optimum
:N_VARIABLES: 2-6
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1-5 general rows + box
:SELECTED_BY: topic-guidance (adversarial review of the gh#484 fixes)
:COVERAGE_TARGET: crates/pounce-qp/src/solver.rs (solve_elastic) — the code changed in f5b1fbc
:END:

Not a single named problem but a generator. Each instance is a QP

\[
\min_x\ \tfrac12 x^\top H x + g^\top x \quad\text{s.t.}\quad b_l \le A x \le b_u,\ x_l \le x \le x_u
\]

built to carry its own verdict, so no solver is needed to know the answer:

- *Feasible instances* fix a witness \(x_w\) in the box and *derive* the row
  bounds from \(A x_w\). Feasibility is then a matter of construction.
- *Infeasible instances* are infeasible by exact arithmetic: either two
  identical rows required to equal different values, or a row required to
  exceed \(\max_{\text{box}} a^\top x\), which for a linear function over a box
  is attained at a corner and so is computable exactly.

`H` is deliberately indefinite with zero diagonal entries — the exact-∇²L
shape that makes the elastic subproblem nonconvex, which is the premise the
certificate needs and does not have. The other knobs reproduce the geometry
that broke it: near-parallel rows (tangency), row scaling by \(10^{\pm6}\)
(γ = 1e6 is the amplifier), witnesses snapped onto bounds (degenerate active
sets), and boxes translated far from the origin (the elastic seed is 0
projected into the box).

** Invariants asserted

1. *Soundness.* `Infeasible` is a Farkas claim. On an instance carrying a
   witness it is false, whatever the numerics did.
2. *Consistency.* `Optimal` at a point violating the problem is a wrong
   answer even when the problem is feasible.
3. *Completeness* (quality, not correctness). On provably infeasible
   instances, `Optimal` is wrong; a non-committal status is merely weaker.
   Tracked separately so that making certification harder shows up as a
   measured cost rather than a silent one.

* Results
:PROPERTIES:
:STATUS: PASS (for the property under test)
:END:

400 instances, seed 20260805 (257 feasible / 143 infeasible). The
generator was extended after the first run to emit *unbounded* boxes —
a free variable is ordinary in an SQP step QP, and it is exactly the case
where the box gives no bound on how far a feasible point can sit — so the
numbers below are on a harder distribution than the first draft's.

|-------------------------------------------+-------------+---------+---------------|
| false `Infeasible` on feasible instances  | 14          | 9       | *0*           |
| `Optimal` at an infeasible point          | 0           | 0       | 0             |
| `Optimal` on a provably infeasible one    | 1           | 1       | *0*           |
| genuine infeasibility certified           | 118/143     | 118/143 | 108/143       |
| solver `Err`                              | 3           | 3       | *0*           |

All three defects are gone. The `Optimal`-on-infeasible case and the three
`Err` cases are *pre-existing* — identical in the first two columns — and
were fixed in the same session; see the Verdict below.

** What round 1 got wrong

Round 1 added a convex feasibility phase-1 so the certificate would rest
on a globally-solved subproblem. That reasoning was right and the fix was
a real improvement, but it left a flaw of its own, which is why 9 cases
survived it.

The phase-1 minimizes `½‖x − r‖² + γ‖v‖₁`. Its proximal term *competes*
with the penalty, so a feasible instance still leaves a residual: `(x̂, 0)`
costs `½‖x̂ − r‖²`, hence

: s* ≤ ‖x̂ − r‖² / (2γ)

With `γ = 1e6` and an ordinary box that ceiling is ~1e-5 — four orders
above `feas_tol`. Instrumenting one failing instance showed the phase-1
returning `status=Optimal` with a residual of `3.45e-6` against a computed
bias ceiling of `2.16e-5`: converging correctly, and stopping exactly where
the model says it must. Round 1 then judged that point against `feas_tol`,
found it wanting, and issued the certificate — reading a penalty artefact
as a Farkas proof.

Two intermediate attempts are worth recording because they *look* right
and are not:

- *Re-centring `r` on the previous iterate.* The bound is quadratic in
  `‖x̂ − r‖`, so this collapses the residual only if the iterate stops
  moving. It does not: successive passes travelled ~1.5 along a
  near-feasible manifold, holding `‖x̂ − r‖` roughly constant. Measured
  3.2e-6 → 1.8e-6 → 3.1e-7, linear at best. Fixed 1 case of 6.
- *Raising the phase-1 iteration budget.* `MaxIter` dominated the residual
  failures, so more iterations looked like the answer. Removing the bump
  entirely changes neither the false-certificate count (0) nor the
  certification rate (108/143) — the γ schedule does all the work. Worse,
  overriding the caller's budget broke a real contract:
  `sqp_qp_max_iter=3` asks for a bounded solve, and a phase-1 quietly
  spending 2000 is not that. The pounce-cli test
  `sqp_qp_options_reach_the_active_set_engine` caught it.

** The cost

108/143 against 118/143. Ten genuine infeasibilities now return a
non-committal status instead of a confident one. That is the price of
demanding a sound proof, and it is the right direction — a false
certificate is a *wrong answer*, a non-committal status is a *weaker* one
— but it is a real cost and it is not tunable: raising the phase-1 budget
5× recovers one of the ten at 3× the wall time, and the free-variable
headroom constant makes no difference at all across three orders of
magnitude (1e1/1e2/1e3 all give 108/143 with 0 false certificates).

* Cross-check / Verification

*The generator was adjudicated before the solver was accused.* Every one of
the 400 instances was re-decided by `scipy.optimize.linprog` (HiGHS) as a pure
LP feasibility question — the objective plays no part in feasibility, so this
is an exact reformulation, not an approximation:

: instances adjudicated: 400
: generator confirmed by scipy: 400
: generator contradicted by scipy: 0
: VERDICT: GENERATOR SOUND

HiGHS agrees with the constructive verdict on all 400, including all six that
pounce certifies infeasible. The generator additionally self-checks each
witness against its own instance before the instance is used.

*Tolerance was ruled out as the explanation.* If the six were merely at the
f64 noise floor, loosening `feas_tol` would clear them. Sweeping it:

| seed        | 1e-9        | 1e-8       | 1e-7       | 1e-6       | 1e-4    |
|-------------+-------------+------------+------------+------------+---------|
| 1899309004  | Infeasible  | Optimal    | Optimal    | Optimal    | Optimal |
| 68471302340 | Infeasible  | Infeasible | Infeasible | Infeasible | Optimal |

Seed 1899309004 is a genuine tolerance-boundary case: one notch looser and it
is fine. Seed 68471302340 is not — it still certifies infeasibility at
`feas_tol = 1e-6`, five orders of magnitude looser than default, on data whose
largest coefficient is 2.73 and largest right-hand side 0.91. No tolerance
argument survives that.

* Verdict

*PASS* for the property under test: no feasible instance in 400 is
certified infeasible, on a distribution deliberately built around the
geometry that broke it.

The two findings this report originally left open were then fixed as well,
taking the probe to a clean sweep: *zero* invariant violations over 400
instances, against 18 when the series began. Neither was a #484 regression
— both reproduce on the pre-#484 code.

- `OptimalOnInfeasible` (seed 36795918414): `solve`'s M5 feasibility audit
  guarded only the `solve_general` branch, so the three cold fast paths
  returned unaudited. Fixed by applying the audit to every path.
- Three `Err` returns: the rank-deficiency prune in
  `cold_general_initial` was single-shot, and the numerical rank test can
  reject its own pruned subset at the next shift. Fixed by iterating the
  prune and falling through to elastic instead of erroring.
