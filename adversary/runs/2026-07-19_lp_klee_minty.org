#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Klee-Minty Cube LP (n = 3..7)
#+DATE: 2026-07-19

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: lp
:CLASS: exponentially-degenerate / badly-scaled LP, closed-form optimum
:SOURCE: V. Klee & G. J. Minty, "How good is the simplex algorithm?", Inequalities III (Shisha ed.), Academic Press 1972, pp. 159-175. Perturbed-cube form as in Chvatal, "Linear Programming" (1983) Ch.4; discussed in Nocedal & Wright 2e Sec. 13.4.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: z* = 100^(n-1) at x* = (0,...,0,100^(n-1))
:N_VARIABLES: n = 3,4,5,6,7
:N_CONSTRAINTS: n inequalities + n nonnegativity bounds
:END:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_{x} \quad & \sum_{j=1}^{n} 10^{\,n-j} x_j \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & 2\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} 10^{\,i-j} x_j + x_i \le 100^{\,i-1}, \quad i=1,\dots,n \\
& x \ge 0
\end{aligned}
\]

The feasible set is a combinatorially perturbed $n$-cube with $2^n$ vertices;
Dantzig-rule simplex visits all of them. For an interior-point method the
adversarial content is not combinatorial but *scaling*: the right-hand side
spans $1 \dots 100^{n-1}$ (up to $10^{12}$ at $n=7$) and the objective
coefficients span $1 \dots 10^{n-1}$. The unique optimal vertex is
$x^\star=(0,\dots,0,100^{n-1})$ with $z^\star = 100^{n-1}$ (verified directly:
the last constraint gives $x_n \le 100^{n-1}$, attained by zeroing all other
$x_j$; any positive $x_j$, $j<n$, costs more in the $2\cdot 10^{i-j}$ coupling
than it gains). pounce minimizes, so $c=-(10^{n-j})_j$ and the expected
objective is $-100^{n-1}$.

Known optimal: $z^\star = 100^{n-1}$ exactly (minimization form $-100^{n-1}$).

Max relative error vs the closed-form optimum across all five sizes:
*4.77e-08*. Scaled solution error $\|x-x^\star\|_\infty / z^\star \le 8.6$e-08.
pounce reports =optimal= at every size and the wall time is essentially flat
(2.5-4.0 ms) as the problem scales by twelve orders of magnitude — the expected
IPM behaviour, immune to the exponential simplex path.

* Cross-check / Verification

Two independent oracles, neither of them pounce:

1. *Closed-form optimum* $100^{n-1}$, derived from the constraint structure and
   independently confirmed by oracle 2 at every $n$.
2. *scipy.optimize.linprog (HiGHS)* — a simplex/vertex method using entirely
   different arithmetic from an IPM. It returns the exact vertex value at all
   five sizes and agrees with pounce to $\le 4.8$e-08 relative.
3. *cvxpy / CLARABEL* — agrees at $n=3,4,5$ (to 7.1e-08). At $n=6$ CLARABEL
   raises =SolverError= outright; at $n=7$ it returns status =unbounded= with
   objective $-\infty$, which is *incorrect* — the problem is bounded with
   optimum $10^{12}$, as HiGHS and the closed form both confirm.

The CLARABEL failure is a limitation of the *oracle*, not evidence about
pounce. It is worth recording in the opposite direction: on the two worst-scaled
instances pounce returned the correct answer where the reference conic IPM
either crashed or reported a false unboundedness certificate.

* Verdict

*PASS.* pounce solves the Klee-Minty cube for $n=3\dots7$ to a maximum relative
objective error of 4.77e-08 versus the exact closed-form optimum, with
=status=optimal= throughout and constant ~3 ms wall time across twelve orders of
magnitude of problem scaling. scipy/HiGHS confirms every value. No
=SOLVER_BUG=, =SOLVER_LIMITATION=, or =PERFORMANCE_REGRESSION=: the absolute
timing gap versus HiGHS is under 3 ms everywhere, far below the 0.1 s threshold.
The only anomaly found belongs to the CLARABEL oracle, which fails at $n=6$ and
returns a false =unbounded= at $n=7$ on a problem pounce handles correctly.
