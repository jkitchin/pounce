#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Active-set cycling / stalling stress battery (degenerate polytopes)
#+DATE: 2026-07-22

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: qp-active-set
:CLASS: massive constraint multiplicity at a vertex; Beale cycling geometry; Slater-failing cone (CQ failure, non-unique multipliers)
:SOURCE: Nocedal & Wright, /Numerical Optimization/ 2e, Sec. 16.5 ("Degeneracy" / cycling in active-set QP); E. M. L. Beale (1955), "Cycling in the dual simplex algorithm", Naval Research Logistics Quarterly 2(4):269-275; standard Slater-failure construction (Boyd & Vandenberghe Sec. 5.2.3)
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: A: 4.0 ; B: -0.05 (LP limit) ; C: 2.5
:N_VARIABLES: A: 8 ; B: 7 ; C: 5
:N_CONSTRAINTS: A: 128 ineq (64 active at x*) ; B: 3 eq + 7 bounds ; C: 40 ineq (ALL 40 active at x*)
:END:

Four instances, all engineered to break active-set pivoting by putting far more
active constraints at the solution than the dimension of the vertex.

** (A) Duplicated / perturbed hypercube
\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^8}\ \tfrac12\|x-2e\|^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
x_i \le 1 - j\cdot 10^{-14},\ \ -x_i \le j\cdot 10^{-14},\quad j=0..7
\]
Every one of the 16 cube facets is replicated 8 times and each replica shifted
by \(j\cdot 10^{-14}\): 128 rows, of which 64 are active at \(x^*=e\).
Replica gradients are *identical*; LICQ fails and the multiplier set is a
56-dimensional face. \(f^*=4\).

** (B) Beale's cycling LP, regularized to a QP
\[
\min\ -0.75x_4 + 150x_5 - 0.02x_6 + 6x_7 + \tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\|x\|^2
\]
s.t. the three Beale equalities and \(x\ge 0\). Swept
\(\varepsilon\in\{10^{-4},10^{-6},10^{-9},10^{-12}\}\) so the QP degenerates to
the pure cycling LP (\(f^*_{LP}=-1/20\) at \(x=(0,0,0,\tfrac1{25},0,1,0)\)).

** (C) Slater-failing polyhedral cone (the instance that broke it)
\[
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^5}\ \tfrac12\|x-e\|^2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad a_i^\top x \le 0,\ i=1..m
\]
with \(a_i\) random unit vectors. For \(m \gtrsim 12\) the positive hull of
\(\{a_i\}\) spans \(\mathbb{R}^5\), so the feasible set collapses to
\(\{0\}\): *all m constraints are active*, there is no interior (Slater fails),
and the multipliers are wildly non-unique. \(x^*=0\), \(f^*=\tfrac12\|e\|^2=2.5\).
Feasibility of \(x=0\) is exact by inspection (\(G\cdot 0 = 0 \le 0\)); the
collapse to \(\{0\}\) was independently verified with cvxpy by maximizing
\(\pm x_i\) over the cone \(\cap\) unit box (extent \(\le 6\times10^{-11}\) for all
\(m\) tested).

* Cross-check / Verification
- *Formulation skepticism first.* The objective is a plain quadratic with
  hand-checked exact gradient/Hessian (\(\nabla f = x-d\), \(\nabla^2 f=I\)).
  Feasibility of \(x=0\) needs no solver: \(G\cdot 0 = 0 \le 0 = h\) exactly.
  So the problem is *provably feasible*, and the claim
  =Infeasible_Problem_Detected= is provably wrong.
- *Independent oracle.* cvxpy/CLARABEL returns 2.4999999996 with
  \(\|x\|_\infty=1.9\times10^{-10}\), matching \(x^*=0\), \(f^*=2.5\). OSQP used
  as a second oracle on instances A/B.
- *Two independent pounce engines disagree with the active-set path.* The
  convex-QP IPM (=solve_qp=) and the general NLP path both return 2.5 with
  \(x\approx 0\) on the identical data, so this is not a data/encoding issue —
  it is specific to the active-set driver.
- *Not a hang, not cycling.* Every solve ran in a child process under a 30 s
  hard wall-clock timeout; nothing timed out. The worst active-set wall time in
  the whole battery was 0.101 s. The two classic cycling geometries (A and
  Beale B/D) were solved cleanly in *one* iteration each, including in the LP
  limit \(\varepsilon=10^{-12}\). The failure mode here is *premature/incorrect
  infeasibility certification*, not stalling.
- *Both entry points affected identically.* =solver_selection="qp-active-set"=
  and =algorithm="active-set-sqp"= produce bit-identical failures, confirming
  the flag really does reach the active-set SQP driver.
- *Performance.* Active-set on C is 0.056 s vs cvxpy 0.0022 s — 25x, but the
  absolute gap is 0.054 s, under the 0.1 s threshold, so not a
  PERFORMANCE_REGRESSION.

* Verdict
*SOLVER_LIMITATION* (instance C). On a convex QP whose feasible set is the
single point \(\{0\}\) — every one of the 40 constraints active at a
5-dimensional point, Slater's condition failing, multipliers non-unique — the
active-set SQP driver reports =Infeasible_Problem_Detected= on a problem that is
provably feasible by inspection and that pounce's own IPM path, pounce's NLP
path, and cvxpy all solve to 1e-10. It does so even when started *at* the exact
optimum with zero constraint violation, and from an interior start it returns
the unmodified starting point (19% objective error, 0.167 constraint violation).
Per the classification rules this is a LIMITATION and not a BUG: =success= is
=False= and the status is never =optimal=, so no wrong answer is claimed as
converged — a caller checking =success= is correctly warned off. The breakdown
threshold is ~20-25 simultaneously active constraints in \(\mathbb{R}^5\)
(\(m/n\gtrsim 5\)); below it the same construction solves correctly.

The headline hypothesis under test — active-set *cycling* — was *not*
reproduced. Instances A (64 duplicated active constraints at an 8-D vertex,
gradients identical to 1e-14) and B/D (Beale's cycling LP down to
\(\varepsilon=10^{-12}\), where pounce recovers \(-1/20\) more accurately than
cvxpy) both terminate in a single iteration with 1e-13 accuracy. The
anti-cycling machinery is sound; what is fragile is the *feasibility
certification* under gross constraint multiplicity.
