* Direction
The companion notebook =notebooks/barrier_curvature_sensitivity.ipynb= formalizes
four notions of sensitivity (A exact map, B finite-mu central path, C inverse
barrier Hessian, D active-set KKT) and the traps an IPM falls into near an active
bound: reporting the finite-mu central-path derivative as exact, over-damping via
the inverse barrier Hessian, and the one-sided / set-valued derivative at a weakly
active bound. QpSensitivity holds the ACTIVE-SET KKT factorization, i.e. it is
*designed* to compute notion D. This iteration checks it actually does — using the
notebook's prescribed oracle (ONE-SIDED FD re-solve) — and that no barrier
curvature leaks in.

* Key confirmations (the notebook's "good" behavior)
- *No barrier-curvature inflation.* For strongly-active and inactive bounds,
  =dx/db= matches the one-sided FD re-solve to 1e-7..1e-9. The tangential
  sensitivity in the coupled 3-var case uses the REDUCED Hessian =W[1:,1:]= (the
  objective's own curvature), confirmed by the 7.8e-10 FD match — it is notion D,
  not the barrier-blended notion B/C. An active bound REMOVES a direction
  (=dx1/db= = 3e-21 ~ 0, zero motion normal to the bound); it adds no curvature.
- *Active-set-stable steps are exact.* While the active set does not change,
  =parametric_step= is exact across arbitrarily large finite steps (err 4.6e-8),
  as an active-set predictor should be.

* The weakly-active limitation (scenario 3) — known, intrinsic, not filed
At a weakly active bound (=x1>=0.5=, solution exactly at the bound, multiplier ~0)
the exact one-sided derivatives DIFFER: releasing the bound gives [0.5,0.5],
holding it gives [0,1]. QpSensitivity returns [0,1] — a VALID subgradient element
(the active branch), accurate for bound-holding perturbations (err 6.6e-5) but
O(delta) wrong for bound-releasing perturbations (err 5.0e-2 at delta=0.1), with
=ill_conditioned=False= and =last_step_residual~1e-20= (the linear solve is clean;
the active set changed, which a linear residual cannot detect). This is the
fundamental active-set-transition one-sidedness — the two-sided derivative does
not exist (notebook Section 1/6), and sIPOPT shares it. Already observed and
noted-not-filed in the sensitivity family (2026-07-19). CANDIDATE ENHANCEMENT
(not filed): expose a weak-activity flag (slack small AND multiplier small), as
the notebook's =diagnose_bounds= does, so a caller knows =dx/db= is one-sided.
