* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: sensitivity (duals / multipliers)
:CLASS: multiplier sign convention, cross-surface audit
:SOURCE: Hillier & Lieberman, /Introduction to Operations Research/, Wyndor Glass Co. LP (Sec. 3.1); shadow prices Sec. 4.7. Plus a bound-active LP with closed-form reduced cost.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: -36 (Wyndor, min form); 5 (bound-active LP)
:N_VARIABLES: 2
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 3 (Wyndor) / 1 + bounds (bound-active LP)
:END:

** Convention used (stated explicitly, since equalities are convention-dependent)

Throughout this report the *AMPL / Pyomo / GAMS marginal-value convention*:

\[ \text{dual}_i \;:=\; \frac{\partial\, f(x^*)}{\partial\, b_i} \]

where \(b_i\) is the right-hand side of constraint \(i\) and \(f\) is the
objective *as stated*.  This definition is identical for equalities and
inequalities, which removes the usual equality ambiguity: an active
\(g_i(x)\le b_i\) row and the corresponding \(g_i(x)=b_i\) row have the
*same* shadow price.  Pyomo's ~model.dual[c]~ from ipopt, GAMS's ~EQU.M~
from CONOPT, and a finite-difference re-solve all realize this
convention, and they were confirmed to agree with each other and with
the analytic values before any pounce surface was examined.

** Problem 1 (Wyndor, min form)

\[
\min\; -3x_1 - 5x_2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad
c_1: x_1 \le 4,\;\; c_2: 2x_2 \le 12,\;\; c_3: 3x_1+2x_2 \le 18,\;\; x\ge 0
\]

\(x^\* = (2,6)\), \(f^\* = -36\), analytic duals \((0,\,-3/2,\,-1)\).
An equality variant replaces \(c_3\) by \(3x_1+2x_2 = 18\) (same
\(x^\*\), same shadow price \(-1\)).

** Problem 2 (bound-active, to probe reduced costs and a =>== row)

\[
\min\; x_1 + 2x_2 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad c_1: x_1+x_2 \ge 3,\;\; 0\le x_1\le 1,\; x_2\ge0
\]

\(x^\*=(1,2)\), \(f^\*=5\), analytic \( \partial f/\partial b_{c_1} = +2 \),
reduced cost of \(x_1\) at its upper bound \( \partial f/\partial u_1 = -1 \).

** Bound-active LP: a =>== row and the reduced costs

| Quantity                                  | correct | pounce  | verdict |
|-------------------------------------------+---------+---------+---------|
| dual of ~c1: x1+x2 >= 3~ (.sol / mult_g)  |      +2 |   *-2*  | flipped |

So the flip is *sign-symmetric*: a =<== row reports \(+1.5\) where
\(-1.5\) is correct, and a =>== row reports \(-2\) where \(+2\) is
correct. It is a pure negation of \(\lambda\), not a magnitude or an
orientation error. *Bound multipliers are NOT affected* — they match
Ipopt exactly and the GAMS reduced cost comes out right.

* Cross-check / Verification

** (a) Is the defect confined to the ~.sol~ writer? — NO.

Solving the *identical* ~.nl~ through the Python library
(~pounce.read_nl~ + ~pounce.Problem.solve~) and through the CLI gives
~info["mult_g"] == .sol duals~ *exactly*, to the last bit, for both the
inequality and the equality model. The ~.sol~ writer
(~crates/pounce-cli/src/nl_writer.rs:221~ ~write_sol_file~, fed at
~crates/pounce-cli/src/main.rs:1379~, ~:1750~ and ~:1923~ — *three*
sites, not two) is a *pure pass-through*: it applies no sign
transformation at all.

The flip therefore lives in *pounce's internal Lagrangian convention*
(\(L = f - \lambda^{\mathsf T} g\), i.e. \(\lambda_{\text{pounce}} =
-\lambda_{\text{Ipopt}}\)), and every surface that exposes ~mult_g~
inherits it. ~crates/pounce-cli/src/qp_extract.rs:221~ ~recover_duals~
reproduces the same convention for the convex/QP path deliberately —
its doc-comment says the inequality sign =z_upper − z_lower= is "fixed
to match POUNCE's NLP path, which is the reference for what a POUNCE
~.sol~ carries", i.e. the flipped convention is *codified as the house
standard*, not a slip in one writer.

Note on ~pounce.minimize~: its ~res.mult_g~ *looks* correct (\(-1.5\),
\(-1\)) but only because ~minimize~ takes constraints in the
\(g(x)\ge 0\) form, which negates the constraint expression relative to
\(g(x)\le b\). Two negations cancel. Solved through the same ~.nl~ the
convention is the flipped one. This is a trap for anyone eyeballing the
API.

** (b) GAMS — a COMPENSATING NEGATION EXISTS. GAMS is CORRECT.

Both GAMS routes negate on output:

- ~gams/gams_pounce.c:1096-1099~ — =/* Negate constraint multipliers:
  POUNCE lambda -> GAMS pi */= then =mult_g[i] = -mult_g[i]= before
  ~gmoSetSolution2~.
- ~python/pounce/gams/link.py:361-365~ — =pi = _to_double_array(gmo,
  -np.asarray(mult_g, dtype=float))=, same comment.

Verified *live* (GAMS 53.2.0, ~option nlp = pounce~): pounce's
~EQU.M~ values match CONOPT's and ipopt's and the analytic shadow
prices, for both the ~=l=~ and the ~=e=~ formulation. The GAMS solve
summary confirms ~SOLVER POUNCE~ / ~MODEL STATUS 2 Locally Optimal~, so
this is not a silent fall-through to another solver.

Variable marginals go out as ~mult_x_L - mult_x_U~ with *no* extra
negation (~gams_pounce.c:1105-1113~, ~link.py:370-377~) and are also
correct — consistent with bound multipliers being unflipped.

*This is the single most important consequence for any fix.* Correcting
the sign at the source (~mult_g~) or in the ~.sol~ writer, without
simultaneously *removing* those two GAMS negations, will silently break
the one surface that is currently right. The two GAMS negations and the
internal flip are a matched pair.

** (c) sIPOPT sensitivity suffix — NOT affected.

~sens_sol_state_1~ is assembled at ~crates/pounce-cli/src/main.rs:1245-1253~
as a ~SolSuffixTarget::Var~ / ~SolSuffixValues::Real~ block whose payload
is ~sens_capture~ — the *perturbed primal* \(x(p+\Delta p)\), per
~crates/pounce-cli/src/sens.rs~. It carries no multipliers, so there is
no sign to get wrong. (The CLI does not accept ~sens_mode=~; the path is
triggered by the ~.nl~ declaring ~sens_state_1~ / ~sens_state_value_1~ /
~sens_init_constr~, per ~crates/pounce-cli/src/cli.rs:276-279~.)

** (d) ~pounce verify~ — self-consistent, but structurally BLIND.

~crates/pounce-cli/src/verify.rs:393-408~ computes the bound-projected
stationarity residual for *both* \(\pm\lambda\) and keeps whichever is
smaller, then reports which it picked ("dual sign +1" / "dual sign -1").
Experimentally it returns rc=0 and =VERDICT: VERIFIED= on:

- pounce's own (flipped) ~.sol~ — reports "dual sign +1";
- ipopt's (correct) ~.sol~ on the same ~.nl~ — reports "dual sign -1";
- a *hand-negated* copy of pounce's ~.sol~ — reports "dual sign -1",
  with a bit-identical stationarity residual (1.177e-10).

So the answer to (d) is *yes, verify would still pass on a
correctly-signed solution* — it is agnostic by construction and cannot
regress-guard the fix. The complementarity check is a magnitude product
and is likewise sign-blind. ~verify~ is not itself broken, but it must
not be relied on to catch this class of defect, and a fix should not
assume ~verify~ will notice a regression.

** (e) Equalities — flipped too, same as inequalities.

Under the stated \(\partial f/\partial b\) convention, the equality
variant of Wyndor has shadow price \(-1\), confirmed by ipopt, CONOPT,
and a pounce finite-difference re-solve of the equality-constrained
~minimize~ model (\(-1.000000\)). pounce's ~.sol~ / ~mult_g~ report
\(+1\). There is no equality/inequality asymmetry: a single global
negation of \(\lambda\) explains every observation.

*AFFECTED (dual sign negated vs. the AMPL/Pyomo/GAMS convention):*
1. ~.sol~ writer, NLP path — ~crates/pounce-cli/src/main.rs:1379~

*NOT AFFECTED:*
- primal \(x\) and the objective value (verified on every surface);
- variable bound multipliers ~mult_x_L~ / ~mult_x_U~ (match Ipopt);
- *GAMS, both routes* — a compensating negation exists and GAMS
  marginals are correct (verified live against CONOPT);
- GAMS variable marginals;
- ~sens_sol_state_1~ (primal-only payload);
- ~pounce verify~ (sign-agnostic; passes either convention).

* Verdict

*SOLVER_BUG* — pounce reports constraint duals negated relative to the
AMPL/Pyomo marginal-value convention \(\partial f/\partial b\), on
every user-facing surface *except* GAMS. Confirmed independently by
analytic shadow prices, a pounce-only finite-difference re-solve, ipopt
via Pyomo, and CONOPT via GAMS, on both a \(\le\) model and a \(\ge\)
model and on an equality variant. The earlier localization to the
~.sol~ writer was too narrow: the writer applies no transformation at
all, and the identical flipped vector is visible in
~info["mult_g"]~ and in the JSON report, so the defect is pounce's
internal Lagrangian sign convention. The scope-critical finding for any
fix is that ~gams/gams_pounce.c:1096-1099~ and
~python/pounce/gams/link.py:361-365~ each apply an explicit compensating
negation, which makes GAMS currently *correct*; those two negations must
be removed in the same change that corrects the internal sign, or the
fix will break the one surface that works. ~pounce verify~ cannot be
used as the regression guard — it tries both signs and accepts either.
