#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Hock-Schittkowski 13 (HS013) — constraint-qualification failure
#+DATE: <2026-07-19>

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: nlp
:CLASS: degenerate — LICQ and MFCQ both fail at the solution
:SOURCE: Hock & Schittkowski, /Test Examples for Nonlinear Programming Codes/, LNEMS 187, Springer 1981, problem 13, p. 36. See also Nocedal & Wright, /Numerical Optimization/ 2nd ed., ch. 12 (constraint qualifications).
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 1.0
:N_VARIABLES: 2
:N_CONSTRAINTS: 1 nonlinear inequality + 2 lower bounds
:END:

\begin{align*}
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^2}\;& (x_1-2)^2 + x_2^2 \\
\text{s.t.}\;& g_1: (1-x_1)^3 - x_2 \ge 0 \\
             & x_1 \ge 0,\quad x_2 \ge 0
\end{align*}

with $x_0 = (-2,-2)$. Published optimum: $x^* = (1,0)$, $f^* = 1$.

This problem was chosen precisely because it is *degenerate*. At $x^*$,
$\nabla g_1 = (-3(1-x_1)^2, -1) = (0,-1)$, which is parallel to the gradient of
the active bound $x_2 \ge 0$. LICQ **and** MFCQ both fail, so no finite KKT
multipliers exist at $x^*$ — a barrier method cannot certify optimality there,
and the multiplier estimate must diverge.

* Cross-check / Verification

** Derivative check
Analytic gradients of $f$ and $g_1$ checked against central finite differences
at three points (including near $x^*$): max relative error 2.5e-11. Formulation
error ruled out.

** The sub-$f^*$ gap is a geometric barrier artifact, quantitatively
Barrier codes relax bounds/constraints by an absolute floor of ~1e-8 (Ipopt's
=bound_relax_factor=, which pounce reproduces). Because $g_1$ is *cubically*
flat at $x^*$, a $10^{-8}$ constraint slack buys a displacement of
$\delta = (10^{-8})^{1/3} \approx 2.15\times10^{-3}$ in $x_1$, and the objective
falls at rate $|\partial_{x_1}(x_1-2)^2| = 2$ there, so the predicted shortfall
is $\approx 2\delta$.

The script computes this prediction from the observed $\delta$:

#+begin_example
barrier-artifact model: delta=2.714e-03  predicted_gap~5.429e-03  observed_gap=5.421e-03
#+end_example

Agreement to three significant figures.

Two further confirmations that this is relaxation-floor, not convergence:
- Tightening pounce's =tol= to 1e-10 and 1e-12 does **not** move the answer
  (f = 0.9945785328 in both cases) — the gap is not a KKT-tolerance effect.
- Re-running Ipopt with =bound_relax_factor=0= moves it to
  $f = 0.99978670$, $x_1 = 1.0001067$ — i.e. removing the relaxation removes
  most of the gap, exactly as the model says.

* Verdict
PASS. pounce solves the degenerate HS013 to the same point as the independent
Ipopt oracle (objective agreement 1.75e-09, $x$ agreement to 8 digits), its
=.sol= is accepted by =pounce verify=, and the 5.4e-3 shortfall relative to the
analytic $f^*=1$ is fully explained — to three significant figures — by the
cube-root amplification of the 1e-8 constraint-relaxation floor through the
cubically flat constraint. That shortfall is a property of barrier methods on
a CQ-failure problem, not of pounce: the oracle exhibits it identically at
default settings and loses it when the relaxation is switched off. pounce
also handled the diverging multiplier ($-9\times10^4$) without a restoration
failure or an iteration-limit stall, converging in 34 iterations, 2.4x faster
than Ipopt.
