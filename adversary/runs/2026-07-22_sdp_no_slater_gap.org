#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Weakly-Feasible SDP with Nonzero Duality Gap (no Slater point)
#+DATE: 2026-07-22

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: sdp
:CLASS: ill-conditioned / weakly feasible (Slater CQ fails, positive duality gap)
:SOURCE: L. Vandenberghe & S. Boyd, "Semidefinite Programming", SIAM Review 38(1):49-95, 1996, Section 4 (Duality) -- the standard positive-duality-gap example (p*=0, d*=-1)
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 0.0 (p*; dual d* = -1, gap = 1)
:N_VARIABLES: 2
:N_CONSTRAINTS: one 3x3 PSD cone (+ one 1x1 PSD cone in the graded variant)
:END:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^2}\quad & x_1\\
\text{s.t.}\quad & F(x)=\begin{bmatrix}0 & x_1 & 0\\ x_1 & x_2 & 0\\ 0&0& x_1+1\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0
\end{aligned}
\]

A PSD matrix with a zero diagonal entry must have that entire row/column zero,
so the leading 2x2 block forces \(x_1=0,\ x_2\ge 0\); the (3,3) entry is then
\(1>0\).  Hence the feasible set is the ray \(\{(0,x_2): x_2\ge 0\}\) and
\(p^\star = 0\), attained on an unbounded optimal face.  \(F(x)\) is *singular
at every feasible point* (rank \(\le 2\) of 3): there is **no Slater point**, no
central path, and the dual optimum is \(d^\star=-1\), a duality gap of 1.

Two variants were run:

- *A (graded)*: the same problem plus \(x_2 \le 1\).  Analytically identical
  (\(p^\star=0\), still no Slater point, still gap 1) but numerically well
  posed, so the cvxpy oracles can actually solve it.
- *B (informational)*: the raw unbounded instance.  In floating point the PSD
  condition \(\det = -x_1^2 \ge 0\) is "satisfiable" to machine precision with
  \(|x_1|\sim\sqrt{\varepsilon x_2}\), so as \(x_2\to\infty\) the instance is
  *numerically* unbounded below.  Not gradable -- see below.

Known optimal: 0.0

pounce lands on \(x=(-6.44\times10^{-8},\,0.9488)\).  The optimal matrix is
maximally ill-conditioned as designed: \(\mathrm{eig}(F(x^\star)) =
(-4.4\times10^{-15},\ 0.949,\ 1.000)\), an eigenvalue spread of
\(2.3\times10^{14}\) with the smallest eigenvalue at the negative side of
machine epsilon -- i.e. pounce converged onto the boundary of the cone where no
strictly feasible point exists, with a PSD violation of \(4.4\times10^{-15}\)
(round-off, not a real violation).  It is the *most* accurate of the three on
this instance, beating CLARABEL slightly and SCS by 3.5 orders of magnitude.

* Cross-check / Verification
- *svec re-derivation*: the layout (lower triangle, column-major, off-diagonals
  \(\times\sqrt2\)) was re-derived and asserted numerically against
  \(\langle X,Y\rangle=\mathrm{svec}(X)\cdot\mathrm{svec}(Y)\) on 5 random
  symmetric pairs, and the affine map \(s = h - Gv\) was checked to reproduce
  \(\mathrm{svec}(F(x))\) exactly at a random \(x\).  Both assertions pass, so
  the encoding is not the source of any discrepancy.
- *Two independent oracles*: SCS and CLARABEL agree with each other (and with
  the analytic \(p^\star=0\)) on variant A to \(<2.5\times10^{-4}\); CLARABEL
  agrees with pounce to \(5\times10^{-8}\).
- *Analytic optimum*: \(p^\star=0\) is provable in closed form (zero-diagonal
  argument), so no reference-value uncertainty.
- *Timing*: pounce 0.0047 s vs CLARABEL 0.0025 s -- a 1.9x gap, but the
  absolute difference is 2.2 ms, far below the 0.1 s threshold, so not a
  performance regression.  pounce is 29x faster than SCS.

* Verdict
*PASS.*  On the well-posed form of the classic Vandenberghe-Boyd
no-Slater/positive-duality-gap SDP, pounce returns =optimal= with objective
\(-6.4\times10^{-8}\) against the analytic \(p^\star=0\), matching CLARABEL to
\(5\times10^{-8}\) and beating SCS by three orders of magnitude, while sitting
on a solution whose PSD matrix has an eigenvalue spread of \(2.3\times10^{14}\).
Slater failure alone therefore does not break pounce's SDP path -- it converges
onto the singular boundary and reports honest status.

The raw unbounded variant B is *not* a pounce finding: it is ill-posed in
floating-point arithmetic (the constraint \(\det=-x_1^2\ge0\) is satisfiable to
round-off for \(|x_1|\lesssim\sqrt{\varepsilon x_2}\), so any solver can drift
to \(-\infty\)), and the oracles fail worse than pounce does -- SCS returns
\(-14.9\) while claiming near-optimality, and CLARABEL raises =SolverError=.
pounce at least reports =iteration_limit= rather than claiming optimality on a
diverging iterate, which is the correct honest behavior.  Per the adversary
rules (oracle must solve it reliably first), no verdict is claimed on B.
