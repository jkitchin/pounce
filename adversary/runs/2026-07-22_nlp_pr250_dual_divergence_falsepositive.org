* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY:   nlp
:CLASS:    termination-guard false positive (differential / regression sweep)
:SOURCE:   MINLPLib =autocorr_bern55-06.nl= (continuous relaxation), via the local corpus at ~/.cache/discopt/minlplib/current/nl
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: -2304.0000278027342 (Ipopt 3.x, confirmed stationary: NLP error 3.7e-9)

Target: **PR #250** / commit =ba29b53=, "Guard against dual-infeasibility
divergence (emfl050 warm-start stall, #246)", which added the option
=dual_diverging_streak= (default 15, 0 disables).

The guard watches the NLP-scaled dual infeasibility and, once it has grown for
15 consecutive iterations in an elevated regime, diverts the solve into the
restoration phase (or terminates =DivergingIterates=). It was added to convert a
permanent stall on =emfl050_5_5= into a solve.

Adversarial hypothesis under test: dual infeasibility legitimately grows for
many consecutive iterations on hard-but-solvable problems, so a fixed
15-iteration streak will fire on solves that would have converged.

* Results

:STATUS: FAIL (confirmed false positive, narrow)
:END:

| Configuration                | Status                    | Objective    | NLP error | Iters |
|------------------------------+---------------------------+--------------+-----------+-------|
| POUNCE head, guard default   | Solved To Acceptable Level | -2263.461245 |   1.0e+00 |   111 |
| POUNCE head, =dual_diverging_streak=0= | Optimal Solution Found | -2304.000028 |   3.7e-09 |    72 |

Guard-disabled POUNCE matches Ipopt iteration-for-iteration (72) and to 12
significant figures. With the guard at its default the solve is diverted into
restoration and ends at a **non-stationary** point: the objective is 40.5 worse
(1.8 %), and the overall NLP error is **1.0** — nine orders of magnitude above
the converged value.

The reported status is =Solved To Acceptable Level= (AMPL =objno 100=). That is
technically defensible, because =acceptable_dual_inf_tol= defaults to 1e10 and
so admits an enormous dual residual, but it overstates the result: a caller
reading "acceptable" gets a point that is feasible (constraint violation 9e-12)
yet not remotely KKT.

* Prevalence

A 500-model corpus sweep of the head binary with the guard at default vs
disabled (same binary both sides):

- guard degrades the outcome: **1 / 500** (=autocorr_bern55-06=)
- guard rescues the outcome:  **2 / 500** (=deb7=, =deb9= — both go
  =objno 100= -> =objno 0=, objective 104.95 -> 97.56 and 104.53 -> 97.56)

So the guard is **net positive** on this corpus and should not be reverted. The
finding is a narrow false-positive case, not a broad regression.

* Verdict

=SOLVER_BUG= (low severity, narrow). The dual-divergence guard fires on a solve
that both Ipopt and pre-#250 POUNCE converge cleanly, and the diverted solve
returns a materially worse, non-stationary point under an "acceptable" status.
The 15-iteration streak plus the internal absolute thresholds are evidently not
quite selective enough to distinguish this model's legitimate dual-infeasibility
transient from =emfl050='s runaway.

Because the guard rescues twice as many models as it breaks, the right response
is to sharpen the firing condition rather than to disable or revert it — for
example by additionally requiring that primal feasibility is *not* improving
(=emfl050='s stall is characterised by the filter admitting steps because primal
feasibility inches down while inf_du runs away), or by re-checking after the
diversion whether the restored point actually improved on the pre-diversion
iterate and rolling back if it did not.
