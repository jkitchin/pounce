#+TITLE: Adversary Run: Robust Linear Programming with Ellipsoidal Uncertainty

* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: socp
:CLASS: SOC-constrained LP (robust optimization -> SOCP)
:SOURCE: Boyd & Vandenberghe, Convex Optimization, Sec 4.4.2 "robust linear programming"
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: none published; certified via KKT stationarity re-derivation (see below)
:N_VARIABLES: 2
:N_CONSTRAINTS: cones=[("nonneg",2), ("soc",3), ("soc",3)]
:SELECTED_BY: log-rotation
:COVERAGE_TARGET: -
:END:

A firm chooses production levels $x=(x_1,x_2)\ge 0$ to maximize profit
$c^Tx = x_1+2x_2$ subject to two resource constraints whose consumption
coefficients are only known to lie in a Euclidean ball around a nominal
value $a_i$ (radius $\rho_i$). The worst case over the ellipsoid gives the
robust reformulation, a genuine SOCP:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{maximize} \quad & x_1 + 2x_2 \\
\text{subject to} \quad & a_1^Tx + \rho_1\|x\|_2 \le b_1, \quad a_1=[1,1],\ \rho_1=0.3,\ b_1=10\\
& a_2^Tx + \rho_2\|x\|_2 \le b_2, \quad a_2=[1,3],\ \rho_2=0.2,\ b_2=15\\
& x \ge 0
\end{aligned}
\]

Both robust constraints bind at the optimum ($g_1(x)\approx 0$,
$g_2(x)\approx 0$), with no bound active ($x>0$ componentwise). pounce's
solution $x^*=[5.377501,\ 2.803214]$ matches cvxpy/CLARABEL to
$x_\infty\text{-err}=1.19\times 10^{-9}$ and cvxpy/SCS to the same order.

* Cross-check / Verification

Two independent cvxpy conic solvers (CLARABEL, SCS at eps=1e-9) build the
identical SOCP from cvxpy's native SOC-representable norm constraints
(no re-derivation of pounce's cone encoding is trusted here — cvxpy handles
its own norm-to-cone lowering). Both agree with pounce to ~1e-9 or better.

Beyond solver-vs-solver agreement, the script re-derives the KKT
stationarity conditions directly from pounce's returned $x^*$ using the
analytic gradients $\nabla g_i(x) = a_i + \rho_i x/\|x\|$ (independent of
any solver's internal dual variables): with both robust constraints active
and no bound active, solving the (2-equation) stationarity system for
$(\mu_1,\mu_2)$ by least squares gives $\mu=[0.2866, 0.5412]$, both
non-negative (dual feasible), and the resulting stationarity residual on
the free coordinates is exactly 0 to machine precision. Since the problem
is convex and Slater's condition holds trivially ($x=0$ is strictly
feasible, as $b_1,b_2>0$), KKT satisfaction here is both necessary and
sufficient for global optimality — a certification independent of pounce,
cvxpy, or any other solver's internal machinery.

* Verdict
PASS — pounce's SOCP solve matches two independent cvxpy backends to
~1e-9-1e-11 and independently satisfies the KKT optimality conditions
re-derived from first principles. No discrepancy found.
