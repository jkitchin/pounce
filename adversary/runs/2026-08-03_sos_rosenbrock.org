The classic Rosenbrock test function:

\[
f(x,y) = (1-x)^2 + 100(y-x^2)^2 = 1 - 2x + x^2 + 100y^2 - 200x^2y + 100x^4
\]

has a unique global minimum $f^*=0$ at $(1,1)$ (both squared terms vanish
only there). By Hilbert's theorem every nonnegative polynomial in $\le 2$
variables of degree $\le 4$ is a sum of squares, so the minimal (order-2)
Lasserre relaxation of this quartic is, in principle, exact.

* Cross-check / Verification

- **Multistart scipy BFGS**: 200 random starts on $[-3,3]^2$ all converge
  to $\approx(1,1)$ with $f\approx 3.7\times10^{-13}$, confirming no lower
  value exists in the search region and matching the known minimizer.
- **Dense grid refutation** (601x601 points on $[-3,3]^2$): minimum value
  0.0 exactly at grid resolution, refuting the existence of any point
  scoring below pounce's bound (a lower bound must never be *invalid*, i.e.
  exceed the true minimum by more than solver tolerance).

At the default tolerance the relaxation returned `lower_bound=3.86e-6`,
`is_exact=False` — a small positive excess over the true optimum 0. This is
**not** classified as a `SOLVER_BUG`: tightening `tol` from the default to
`1e-10` shrinks the excess to `8.67e-10`, i.e. it scales with the requested
solve tolerance, and matches the previously logged precedent
(`2026-07-23`, Beale degree-8 SOS probe: `lower_bound=+5.31e-7,
is_exact=False`, filed as "within SDP tolerance of 0, not a finding").
Rosenbrock's famously ill-conditioned valley near $(1,1)$ plausibly also
makes the flat-extension rank-1 certificate ($\mathtt{is\_exact}$) hard to
detect numerically even though the relaxation is theoretically exact by
Hilbert's theorem — a conditioning artifact, not a soundness defect.
`is_exact=False` means `minimizers` is empty, so no minimizer-recovery
check applies (correctly skipped per the family's verdict rule).
