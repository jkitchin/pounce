* Problem
:PROPERTIES:
:FAMILY: sos
:CLASS: unconstrained degree-6 bivariate polynomial, deliberately non-SOS; option-space extension on the ``certified`` lower-bound contract
:SOURCE: T. Motzkin (1967); Reznick, "Some concrete aspects of Hilbert's 17th problem" (2000) Sec 2; Parrilo thesis (2000) Ex. 4.2. M(x,y)=x^4y^2+x^2y^4-3x^2y^2+1.
:KNOWN_OPTIMAL: 0.0, at (x,y) in {(1,1),(1,-1),(-1,1),(-1,-1)}

:N_CONSTRAINTS: 0 (primary probe, unconstrained); 4 box inequalities (extension)
:SELECTED_BY: log-rotation
:COVERAGE_TARGET: -

The Motzkin polynomial is the textbook example of a polynomial that is
nonnegative everywhere (by AM-GM) but is **not** expressible as a sum of
squares — it sits exactly on the boundary of the SOS cone. This makes it a
deliberately adversarial input for a Lasserre-hierarchy solver: the
relaxation is not required to be tight at low order (a loose-but-valid
bound is expected, not a bug), but the bound must never *exceed* the true
global minimum (0.0) when the solver claims soundness.

All 8 =certified=True= bounds are sound (<=0) across 4 orders of magnitude
of =tol=. At order=4, tol=1e-8, the bound is essentially exact
(-1.37e-6, =is_exact=True=).

The contrasting *uncertified* failure mode was independently confirmed in a
side probe (unconstrained, order=4, tol=1e-3): pounce reported
=status=optimal, lower_bound=0.00292, certified=False= — a raw SDP value
that lands slightly *above* the true minimum, exactly the documented
uncertified failure mode ("normally correct to several digits but can land
slightly above the true minimum"). Because =certified=False= there, this is
not a violation of any contract — the field correctly told the caller not
to trust the bound as a rigorous lower bound.

* Cross-check / Verification
Grid refutation (801x801 over [-2,2]^2) and 300-start scipy BFGS both
independently confirm the global minimum is 0.0 at (±1,±1). pounce's
=is_exact=True= case (order=4, boxed, tol=1e-8) recovers a bound within
1.37e-6 of the true value. All =certified=True= bounds across the sweep
respect soundness; the one unsound raw value observed was correctly flagged
=certified=False=.

* Verdict
PASS on both the primary probe and the option-space extension. pounce's SOS
solver behaves exactly as documented on a deliberately adversarial,
famous non-SOS-but-nonnegative polynomial: loose-but-valid bounds when
unconstrained/uncertified (expected), and rigorously sound bounds whenever
=certified=True=, even under 4 orders of magnitude of tolerance loosening.
No defect found; the =certified=/=uncertified= distinction in
=SosResult= is confirmed load-bearing, not decorative.
